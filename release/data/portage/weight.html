02 · The body, under load

183%

More than their own weight.

Nepalese porters carry up to 183 % of their own body mass (Minetti et al. 2006). The Sichuan tea porters photographed by E. H. Wilson in 1908 carried bales whose loads de Rosthorn gave in 1895 as 250–360 lb, about 113–163 kg. The standard load of a Mesoamerican tlamemeh was about 23 kg; an East African caravan load, about 30 kg (Rockel).

The studio’s unit asks for none of that: about 2.6 kg, carried by choice.

03 · Paris, 1901 · the entrance test

200 kg over 60 m.

To join the forts des Halles, the porters of the Paris market, a candidate had to carry a load across the hall. The print records the test; later accounts give the load as 200 kg, carried over 60 m, “the length of a pavilion”.

04 · The gait · how a load is walked

The load becomes part of the stride.

In 1986 Maloiy, Heglund and colleagues found that Luo and Kikuyu women carried head and tumpline loads of up to 20 % of their body weight at no rise in energy cost (Nature 319). In 1995 Heglund and colleagues showed why: the carriers exchange potential and kinetic energy more completely at each step, so the body works as a better pendulum (Nature 375). Bastien and colleagues (2005) measured the same kind of economy in Nepalese porters, who carry loads more cheaply than untrained walkers (Science 308).

The tumpline, the frame and the shoulder pad are older than any of these papers. Protagoras, a porter, was said to have invented the pad (Diogenes Laertius IX.53).

05 · The unit · 8 × 8 × 24 in

The weight is in the surface.

The unit is a foam core skimmed with plaster: the Armenian Flag Stack’s 8 × 8 × 24 in, 25.17 litres. The core is most of the volume and a fifth of the weight. At a 3 mm skim, about 80 % of the weight is in the skin. It looks like stone because of a few millimetres. A solid plaster block the same size would weigh about 30 kg, twelve times as much.
