IV · The Machine · 1846 – now

The bridge refuses the wheel.

Step IV of V

The railway came in 1846 and the road in 1933, and both stopped at the edge of the city. Inside, every load still crosses the bridges by boat, by hand truck or on a back. This sheet follows what replaced the porter, and where it could not reach.

1846
01 · The railway

The iron road reaches the lagoon.

The railway bridge from the mainland opened in 1846. Goods could now come to Venice on wheels as far as the station, and no further: from the platform onward, everything still went by boat or on a back.

On the stage and in print the porter had long been a figure of fun and pity; in the 19th century the machine began to make him a figure of the past.

1933 · 02 · Piazzale Roma · km 0.0

The road ends at Piazzale Roma.

The road bridge, Ponte Littorio, now Ponte della Libertà, opened in 1933 and ends at Piazzale Roma, the city’s only vehicle access. It is where the studio’s walk will begin.

The first stairs.

A traveller off the train or the bus meets a bridge within minutes. The Scalzi climbs in several flights of 4–7 steps; Calatrava’s Ponte della Costituzione beside Piazzale Roma, 51–52 steps a flight (OSM step_count).

Route A’s first crossing, Papadopoli, is 91 m from the start: 12 up, 12 down.

03 · Inside the city · no wheel crosses unlifted

Boat, hand truck, back.

The barge is the city’s lorry. From the quay, the hand truck takes over, and at every bridge it has to be hauled up the steps, one at a time. The stair-climbing hand truck is the last tool; the studio goes one step further back, to the frame on the shoulders.

2025
04 · The porters still there

A licensed trade, still.

In April 2025 the historic cooperative of the Portabagagli del Porto di Venezia won the cruise terminal’s luggage contract. The porter did not vanish; he moved to the edges the machine still cannot cross, and he still works by contract and licence, as the bastazi did.

In Genoa, the Compagnia Caravana of Bergamasque porters, chartered in 1340, passed into the dock workers’ company, the CULMV, in 1946.

05 · Elsewhere · the back still carries

Where the machine never arrived.

On mountain paths, in markets and on old bridges, the load still goes on a back or a head. Nepalese porters carry up to 183 % of their body mass. In Accra, the kayayei carry goods on their heads through the markets.

The Istanbul hamals and the Armenian porters of Constantinople are on III The World.

1861
06 · A place to set it down

The Porters’ Rest, Piccadilly.

In 1861 London put up a shelf at shoulder height in Piccadilly, “for the benefit of porters and others carrying burdens”, so a load could be set down and taken up again without lifting it from the ground. It still stands, a monument to a trade that no longer needs it.

The studio’s walk will need its own rests: on landings, on the parapets of the bridges.
