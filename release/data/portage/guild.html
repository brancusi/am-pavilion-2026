Sheet II · The Guild · 1413 → 1797

Carrying, licensed, inherited, sold.

Step II of V

For four centuries carrying in Venice was not a free-for-all. It was licensed, inherited, sold and blessed. The men who did it came down from the Bergamasque valleys, and one of them left 1,200,000 ducats.

1340 – 1598
01 · From the valleys

Asses or pack mules.

“Sent out of their valleys for the benefit of the whole world, which uses them like asses or pack mules.”

Tomaso Garzoni, La piazza universale, Venice, 1585, on the Bergamasque porters (tr. here).

Vecellio, in 1590, says what they did: they “load and unload ships and boats and carry goods from one place to another on their backs. Most of them are Bergamasques … and Brescians” (tr. here). The sacking over the head is a linen sack, worn as a hood under the load.

11 June 1340Genoa, the Caravana statute“Niuno presumi di venir ammesso nella Caravana, se non sia di Bergamo”: no one may join unless he is from Bergamo. Wives went home to the valley to give birth, so their sons could succeed.
1628Venice, the Dogana da MarMembers only from Dossena and Sorisole, in the Val Brembana.

8 June 1413
02 · The companies

An altar, a patron, a door.

On 8 June 1413 the porters of the Fontego dei Tedeschi, the Germans’ warehouse at Rialto, founded their company. They kept the first altar on the left at San Bortolomio, under St Nicholas. In 1785 there were five of them, still keeping their feast “with dignity”.

The companies, as far as they are documented.

1454 – 1837
03 · Duties and feasts

The Doge’s melons.

For a new Doge, porters “in smocks of cloth printed with red flowers” carried a gilded basket of melons and a platform with St Josaphat (Paoletti, 1837; tr. here). The privilege came with duties.

1454Every porter registered with the Council of Ten, to be called out to fight fires
c. 175424 soldi a month to the Arsenal, or two days’ unpaid work there each month (Grevembroch)
1837Paoletti records the melons and the flowered smocks for a new Doge

1628 – 1694
04 · The bought place

We alone.

Negri co ne vedè privileggiai
Semo nù soli in portar via carbon

Black as you see us, we are privileged: we alone may carry the charcoal away.

The verse under Zompini’s plate, 1753 (tr. here).

A place to carry was property. It could be inherited, closed to outsiders, and sold.

25corbe, charcoal baskets, sold by the Republic: the number of charcoal porters could not change

1634 · 1646
05 · Porter to patrician

1,200,000 ducats.

Giovanni Widmann was “da principio bastaso, o facchino, nel Fondaco dei Tedeschi”: at first a porter in the Fondaco. He died in 1634 leaving 1,200,000 ducats, and in 1646 his sons were made Venetian nobles (Tassini).

“Fazzo el facchin per desgrazia; ma son fiol de una persona civil.”

“I’m a porter by bad luck, but I’m the son of a respectable man.”

The Venetian porter in Carlo Goldoni, Il servitore di due padroni, 1745/53, Act I, sc. 7 (tr. here). Fortune on one side; on the other, the stage’s porter and his pride.

1585 – 1753
07 · The stage

Zanni.

The Bergamasque porter became a mask. Zanni, the servant of the commedia, began as his caricature. Winifred Smith, in 1912, notes that the link “is apt only in Venice where the peasant porters were unpopular”.

Garzoni gives the porters’ own reading of their name: “Fachino si dimanda, perche fa riverenza, & inchino alle persone”, he is called facchino because he bows. Others read fa chino, he goes bent. One posture, two meanings: see The Burden.

1797
08 · The end of the Republic

The Republic falls.

The Republic that sold the baskets and granted the places ends in 1797. Twelve years earlier the Fondaco’s company was down to five men, still keeping their feast. The porter goes on, out into the world: movement III.
