The Studio · 1975 → 2026

The studio and the Armenian Pavilion.

The unit is what the studio carries. The pavilion at Tesa 41, Arsenale, is where the walk ends.

The unit · 8 × 8 × 24 in

The unit.

The studio casts light units: a core of EPS foam under a thin skin of pigmented plaster. They look like masonry and weigh about 2.6 kg each.

Most of that weight is in the skin, about 80 % at a 3 mm skim. A solid plaster block of the same size would weigh about 30 kg.

The walk’s unit is planned at the Flag Stack’s size, 8 × 8 × 24 in (20.3 × 20.3 × 61.0 cm).

The unit runs through the studio’s other studies of 2026: slabs, posts, fins and cubes that stack, lean and are set down again.

2026-006 · Armenian Flag Stack

Eighty units, one tower.

Armenian Flag Stack (2026-006) is a tower built from units small enough for one person to lift. Version 1 has 80 units in 10 courses, 40 × 40 × 80 in. Version 2 opens it: 40 units, each course a ring of four with an 8 in gap on every face, so the stripes spiral in colour and in space. Version 3 continues to 18 courses, 72 units, 144 in.

Stacking is carrying made permanent. The tower is a load set down.

Version 1 would weigh about 207 kg, roughly one fort des Halles test load (200 kg over 60 m). The work’s weight is in the distance, the steps and the repetition, not the kilograms.

Units, stacked and leaned.

Units, stacked and leaned.

Most of the studio’s 2026 studies are built from a few plain units: slab, post, fin, cube. Some stack into a tower, some lean on one another, some lie side by side on a base.

Each is a configuration and a rendering before it is a cast, and several already have fabrication sheets for their moulds. They run from a 10 in study to a 23 ft row; only the walk’s unit is sized for one person to carry.

03 · Units, stacked and leaned

One configuration, eleven ways.

One configuration, eleven ways.

The same four units, rebuilt eleven times. The colours, the depth and the size change, and the arrangement holds.

A modular work can be taken apart, carried and put together again.

1975 · New York

Units since 1975.

The unit is not new to the studio. In 1975 Zadik Zadikian gilded plaster bricks with 24-karat gold leaf, stacked them on a New York sidewalk and loaded them on a cart.

In 1978 a thousand gilded bricks were shown at the Tony Shafrazi Gallery in Tehran, on the eve of the Revolution; they were later looted and lost.

Tesa 41 · Arsenale

The Studio, at Tesa 41.

The Studio is the Armenian Pavilion at Biennale Arte 2026, in Tesa 41 of the Arsenale. Tesa 41 (with Tesa 42) is on the Canale delle Galeazze, about 480 m², with its own access from the public road and from the water.

In the hall: Big Red Bed Where Dreams Come True, Who’s Afraid of Red, Yellow and Blue? (a triptych, homage to Barnett Newman), Leaning Red and a pink block with its units, all of plaster, acrylic, pure pigment and EPS foam, 2026.

Km 3.0 · La Celestia

Where the walk ends.

Route A runs from Piazzale Roma, where the road ends, to Campo della Celestia, the public edge nearest the Canale delle Galeazze: 3.0 km, 13 bridges, 378 steps (182 up), about 24–29 m climbed.

Ughi’s plan of 1729 names the Canal delle Galeazze and La Celestia. De’ Barbari engraved “celestia” in 1500.

Since 1717, when the Venetian Senate gave them the island of San Lazzaro, Armenian monks have lived in this lagoon.

2026 · the pavilion works

Three works on the stage.

The studio’s other works for the pavilion were rendered on one shared stage: 7 Steps to Heaven (2026-001), a staircase as a sculpture; Hexagonal Threshold (2026-002); Jasper Johns’ American Flag (2026-003). These are renderings, not photographs.

Venice’s bridges are the staircase’s public cousins: stairs everyone must climb.
