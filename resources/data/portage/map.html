Sheet 6 · The Map · 1500 → 2026

Venice as an obstacle course.

Two engraved plans and one computed walk. De’ Barbari drew the city in 1500, Ughi measured it in 1729, and OpenStreetMap lists it today, stair by stair. On all three, rings mark the same places: where the porters stood, and where the walk goes.

Rings and lettered labels are drawn over the prints. Prints are never tinted.

De’ Barbari engraved names into the view: “Fontico dalaman”, “celestia”, “ARSENAL”, “DVANA DE M”. Where he wrote a name, the ring sits on it; other places are read from their position.

Every bridge a stair

Up one side, down the other.

Zompini etched the street trades of Venice in 1753. Behind his seller of sponges and oranges, a water-carrier takes the steps of a bridge with a yoke and a bucket. The map counts the stairs; the print shows what they cost.

1729 · the plan

1729: the city measured.

Ughi drew Venice from straight above and lettered it: “Riua del Vin”, “Riua del Carbon”, “Fontico de Todeschi”, “S. Bortolamio”, “Dogana da Mare”, “Canal delle Galeazze”. Every ring on this plan sits on an engraved name.

Two things on the route did not exist yet. Piazzale Roma was built in the 1930s, by the “Isola di S. Chiara” (B). Tesa 41 now stands on the Canal delle Galeazze (M).

The two quays

The charcoal came up the Riva del Carbon.

“Black as you see us, we are privileged: we alone may carry the charcoal away” (Zompini’s verse, tr. here). There were 25 of them, and the number could not change, because the Republic had sold the corbe, that is, the right to carry charcoal in them (Tassini; tr. here).

How many bridges

About 400, and nobody agrees.

No count is official. Sources give 391, 403 with the Giudecca, “more than 400”, 417 with 72 private, 435 or 436. They differ on what counts: private bridges, the Giudecca, bridges that adjoin, bridges with one name and two spans.

Bridges for a day

The votive bridges.

Twice a year the city builds a bridge where there is none. For the Redentore, a bridge of boats has crossed to the Giudecca every July since 1577 (1578 by some accounts), today about 330 m from the Zattere. Each 21 November a second one crosses the Grand Canal to the Salute.

A bridge, here, is something people make, carry and take down again.

Km 0.0 · the edge

The city that cannot be wheeled.

The railway reached Venice in 1846, the road in 1933. Both stop at the edge; the road ends at Piazzale Roma. Inside, the bridge refuses the wheel: barges bring the load to the quay, and the last stretch goes by hand truck, up one flight and down the next.

In 2014 the city proposed fines of up to €500 for hard wheels, aimed also at delivery trolleys, then denied a ban. In 2025 the historic Portabagagli del Porto di Venezia won the cruise terminal’s luggage contract.
