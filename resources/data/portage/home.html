Km 0.0 · Piazzale Roma

The wheel stops here.

The railway bridge reached Venice in 1846, the road bridge in 1933. The road ends at Piazzale Roma, still the only place a vehicle can enter the city. From this square on, whatever moves goes by boat or by hand. The stair-climbing hand truck is the last tool; the studio goes one step further back, to a frame on the shoulders.

The route begins here because the road does.

Km 1.65 · Bridge 8

Rialto, in 1500.

Locator: the detail’s place in the whole woodcut (box ours).

In 1500 the Rialto bridge is still wood, with a drawbridge in the middle: the only bridge over the Grand Canal. Beside it stands the Fondaco dei Tedeschi, the German merchants’ house.

Today the stone bridge is the walk’s summit: 18 and 42 steps up, 42 and 12 down.

Km 1.66 · at the Fondaco’s door

fa chino,
fa inchino.

He goes bent; he bows. Garzoni gives both readings of facchino, the porter, in 1585: the Bergamasques said he is called so “because he makes reverence, and an inchino, a bow, to people” (tr. here). One posture, two meanings.

Here the bastazi of the Fontego had their company from 8 June 1413, and their altar at San Bortolomio, patron St Nicholas. One of them, Giovanni Widmann, began as “bastaso, o facchino, nel Fondaco dei Tedeschi”; he died in 1634 leaving 1,200,000 ducats, and his sons were made nobles in 1646.

Km 3.0 · La Celestia

The end of the line.

Tesa 41, with Tesa 42, stands on the Canale delle Galeazze in the Arsenale: about 480 m², with its own access from the public road and by water.

Ughi’s plan of 1729 already names the canal, and the convent of La Celestia beside it. The walk ends on the Campo della Celestia, the public edge nearest the canal.

Since 1717, when the Venetian Senate gave them the island of San Lazzaro, Armenian monks have lived in this lagoon.

The units look like masonry and weigh about 2.6 kg each. The load is light on purpose: the work’s weight is in the distance, the steps and the repetition.
