Armenianas well.

Two Armenians met in Richard Serra's studio in 1973. Serra introduced one to the other as “Armenian as well.” Fifty-three years later they are working side by side again: from Serra's black drawings, through a Tehran gallery lost to a revolution, to a working studio inside the Venice Arsenale.

19732026
Scroll
In brief

Not an outsider rediscovered. An insider whose work was built to disappear.

In the 1970s Zadik Zadikian stood inside several decisive moves of New York sculpture: in Richard Serra’s studio, on the walls at Castelli, at the opening of both of Tony Shafrazi’s galleries. The Studio, in Venice in 2026, continues that line.

Five facts
01

Richard Serra titled a 1974 installation drawing Zadikians. It is in the Guggenheim, Panza Collection, 91.3874.

02

Serra said in print that Abstract Slavery (1974) was the “collective effort of Zadik Zadikian, Tony Shafrazi, and myself.”

03

Zadikian opened both of Shafrazi's galleries: Tehran, 31 Oct 1978, its first and only show; New York, 16 Oct 1979, the gallery that went on to launch Keith Haring and stage Warhol–Basquiat.

04

He made whole-room works downtown: all of 112 Greene Street painted yellow; a studio, then the entrance of P.S.1, covered in gold.

05

The unit runs from 1978 to 2026: Gold Bricks → hand-cast bricks → Path to Nine → Made in USA → The Studio.

The two

A swimmer and a provocateur.

Zadikian

Zadik Zadikian

Sculptor · born in Yerevan, Soviet Armenia

  • A child of Genocide survivors, raised on the Nork heights in Nork-Marash, the district founded by refugees from Marash.
  • Father a builder who cast concrete villas; mother a seamstress.
  • Children's Palace sculpture department; the Panos Terlemezian school; youngest student at the Arts Academy.
  • His materials: paintstick labour, yellow paint, gold foil, then the hand-cast plaster unit.

“Through my art, I was able to survive.” Canvas, 2026

Shafrazi

Tony Shafrazi

Artist, dealer, curator · b. 8 May 1943, Abadan, Iran, to Armenian parents

  • Sent to England at thirteen; Hammersmith, then the Royal College of Art, 1963–67.
  • 1965: first trip to New York. The YMCA opposite Warhol's Silver Factory; Warhol, Lichtenstein and Castelli within about 48 hours.
  • From 1968/69 in New York, teaching at SVA; a regular at the Max's Kansas City front table with LeWitt, Andre and Smithson.

“Maximal minimalism.” His phrase for Gold Bricks · artnet, 2009

I.1
Under the mountain
Yerevan · to 1965

He was born into a capital being rebuilt in pink tuff under Stalin, with the national mountain always in view and always on the other side of the Turkish border.

He grows up on the Nork heights above the centre, in Nork-Marash, the neighbourhood he calls “Marash”, founded by refugees from Marash (Kahramanmaraş). He is a child of genocide survivors. His father is a builder who casts concrete villas.

“I was so new in the country, coming from the Soviet regime, that was anti-Western culture altogether.”

Zadikian, on arriving in the West

I.3
The border
The Aras · 26 November 1965
26 Nov 1965

Zadikian and four friends swim the Aras River from Soviet Armenia into Turkey. Two are shot, one is caught, two get across. He carries the group’s machine gun, about seven pounds, and has to let it go mid-river.

“Inside of the Soviet border, flies even cannot fly.”

Zadikian

A captain opens the gate at about half past ten, believing they are cutting bamboo for his roof. It is a kilometre to the river: three or four hundred metres wide, iced at the banks, in big waves. He lies in the mud while machine-gun fire cuts the fifteen-foot cane above him, then crawls “on four legs, almost like a dog”. A second channel; a friend calls his name; they strip and swim.

“I couldn't talk for half an hour because it was so cold. My whole nervous system, everything was just frozen.”

Zadikian, Venice interview, 2026

Scene · late May 1973

A beautiful day. He takes the subway to the Met and walks all the way down to Tribeca. Fifth-floor loft. He is a friend of Serra's brother Rudy, from San Francisco.

“He opens up the door, bushy hair like this… He says, ‘Who are you?’”

Zadikian

The studio is empty, so Serra sends him to Castelli, where he sits at a table going through the photo files. “I checked all his work. And I loved everything I saw.” Then Serra hires him to build walls, and to knock them down and build another, “so that I would be able to get paid… but hang around him.”

Apprenticeship as a condition, not a job.

1974 · black paintstick on Belgian linen

Drawing leaves the sheet.

“Why don't you make your paper become your figure, and the wall become the ground?”

Zadikian, to Serra

A five-foot sheet stapled to the wall and covered in black chalk. “Okay, I got it.” Two hundred dollars for Belgian linen, gesso, rabbit-skin glue and oil sticks from Canal Street. Serra, with a bad back, directs from a chair. A two-by-four is dragged across the wet surface to take the hand out.

“My God, Richard, what the hell is going on? It's like abstract slavery.”

Shafrazi, on seeing the drawing

The Room

1973 — 1978

A room painted yellow, end to end.

112 Greene Street, SoHo · 1973–74
  1. Paint the entire space yellow, fixtures and furniture included.
  2. Pull the painted-over windows out of their frames.
  3. Open the doors, so the place can breathe.
  4. Make no announcement.

The work, written as instructions. “All of the downtown… art world came through.”

“Why don't you just alter the given space… turn it into the painting.”

Zadikian

He links it to his father’s open concrete shells in Yerevan, “almost like a Gordon Matta-Clark kind of a thing.” Serra’s verdict: “You hit on something by doing that.”

Brooklyn · a proposal

Gild the way in.

The idea was Tony Shafrazi’s: to gild the brick arches of the Brooklyn Museum’s entrance walkway. His proposal led to Zadikian’s inclusion in Solid Gold. The short time frame and the logistics kept the entrance from being done then; the project remains open.

Forty-seven years apart

The same two men. The same gold unit between them.

Tesa 41 · Arsenale · 9 May–22 November 2026

The studio as the work.

For the full seven months of the Biennale, Tesa 41 is a working sculpture studio. Units are cast, pigmented through their full depth, sometimes gilded, stacked, taken down and restacked.

“The act of making is not staged, but neither is it hidden.”

Carlo McCormick, pavilion essay, 2026

“I was doing this with Richard, and he's doing it with me.”

Zadikian, on Shafrazi drawing while he builds · Venice, 2026

In Serra's studio in 1974 the work was made by three people and named after two of them. In Venice the roles have turned: Shafrazi draws, Zadikian builds, and the assistants include his son, Aram. “It doesn't matter if it's coming from me or from Annie or from Tony or from Aram.”
